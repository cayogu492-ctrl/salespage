Case files

Three strong applicants. Three preventable losses.

These composite accounts are built from patterns that repeat every cycle — not because anyone lacked what it takes, but because nobody handed them the one specific detail that would have stopped it.

Preventable
FILE — "It Was One Letter"

Three subject areas. One recycled letter.

A strong Bachelor's-track applicant applied to three subject areas she was genuinely qualified for — then, pressed for time, wrote one motivation letter and swapped the subject name for the other two. All three came back with flat scores. Nothing about her achievements was ever in question.

The fix in the manual: a letter-by-letter framework built for three separate blank pages, not one page wearing three labels.
Preventable
FILE — "The Passport Didn't Match"

One dropped middle name. Weeks of delay.

An applicant registered using the name on his WAEC result — which dropped a middle name his passport's machine-readable zone included in full. He didn't notice until a document check flagged the mismatch, well after the registration deadline had closed.

The fix in the manual: the two-minute passport-to-form check, done before you type a single field.
Preventable
FILE — "The Agent Who Knew Everything"

Real details. Fake access. Real fee.

A Master's-track applicant was contacted by someone claiming to be a former reviewer who could "pre-check" portfolios for a fee. He knew enough real details to sound credible. Her actual portfolio moved forward on its own merit regardless — his "review" cost her two weeks of trusting the wrong voice.

The fix in the manual: the eight-point scam-agent checklist — run any message against it before you reply.

These three accounts are fictional composites built for this book to illustrate common, preventable mistakes — not real applicants.
